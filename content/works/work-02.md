:::chapter The Road South
:::summary
Kaer Morhen is behind them. Everything else is ahead.
:::end-summary

The Witcher's Path ran south in two directions: toward Temeria and the forest roads Geralt knew well enough to walk blindfolded, or through the mountains into territory that belonged to no king who was currently still breathing. They took the mountains.

"If someone is trying to kill her," Geralt said, "they'll expect the known roads."

"If someone is trying to kill her," Yennefer said, without looking up from the map she was refolding with precise, unhappy efficiency, "they'll also expect three people in the mountains in the middle of winter."

"Four," Jaskier said from the back of the group, where he'd been strategically positioned since they left the keep. "I'm here."

Nobody acknowledged this.

Ciri rode between Geralt and Yennefer, which felt slightly like being between two weather systems that had decided not to resolve into storm. She'd been watching them for three days. The way they didn't quite look at each other. The way the not-looking was more loaded than looking would have been.

"You two are weird," she said.

"We're focused," Geralt said.

"You're weird," Ciri repeated, with the confidence of someone who had been through enough to trust her own perceptions. "But I don't mind."

Yennefer made a sound that was not quite a laugh. Geralt's jaw did something complicated.

The road south was frozen solid and the sky overhead was the particular gray of a day that couldn't decide whether to snow. Jaskier began composing something under his breath. It seemed to involve a lot of rhymes for "Witcher."

Geralt said nothing. Yennefer said nothing. But in the inch of space between their horses, something shifted — barely perceptible, like the first crack in ice before the thaw.

:::chapter In Which They Kill a Harpy and Have a Conversation About It Afterwards

The harpy was the third one this week.

"They're nesting early," Geralt said, pulling his silver sword free with the practiced efficiency of someone who had done this ten thousand times and found it tedious rather than alarming. "Climate. Magic. Something changed in the area."

"The Nilfgaardian border moved," Yennefer said. She was standing twenty feet away, having incinerated two of them with a force bolt before they got close enough to be interesting. Her violet eyes were doing the thing they did when she was cataloguing information — slightly unfocused, slightly cold, a sorceress filing facts. "Displaced settlements, burned farmland. It disrupts nesting patterns."

Ciri had killed one herself. Geralt had watched from the corner of his eye while pretending not to watch, and the blade work was sound. Rough at the edges but she knew what she was doing.

"Good kill," he said.

Ciri blinked like she'd expected criticism. "The grip was wrong."

"The grip was fine. You're alive. The harpy isn't." He crouched to clean his blade on the grass. "Wrong grip would've gotten you pulled into the air. You adjusted."

Something in her settled — not pride exactly, but something like the start of it.

Yennefer drifted over. She examined Ciri's form with the same cataloguing expression, and then, without fanfare: "Your footwork was good. Keep that."

Ciri looked between them like she was waiting for the catch.

There wasn't one. Geralt watched Yennefer already walking away, hands gathered in her sleeves, posture saying clearly that the analysis was complete and she had moved on to other concerns. He watched Ciri watch Yennefer.

"She means it," he said quietly. "When she says something's good, she means it."

"Is she always like that?"

Geralt considered this for longer than was strictly necessary.

"She's—" He stopped. Tried again. "She's honest. It doesn't look like kindness but it is, usually."

Ciri turned this over. "What does kindness look like when it does look like kindness?"

He didn't have an answer for that. He thought about the way Yennefer had said *the grip was fine, you're alive*, which was exactly the sentence he would have chosen, and felt something that he'd been declining to examine closely begin to press against the inside of his chest.

"Ask her," he said. "She'll tell you."

:::end-chapter

:::chapter Novigrad, an Inn, and Two People Who Know Better

The inn had one room available.

"There's a stable," the innkeeper offered.

"There's a room," Yennefer said pleasantly, in a tone that suggested the stable was no longer being offered as an option. The innkeeper found a second cot from somewhere. Jaskier immediately claimed it, citing his back. Ciri took the bed. Geralt and Yennefer looked at each other across the remaining floor space.

"I can sleep outside," he said.

"It's raining."

"I've slept in rain."

"Geralt." She said his name with a particular quality — not soft, never soft, but something that meant *stop performing*. "Lie down."

He lay down. She sat against the wall with a candle and whatever she was reading. Ciri was already asleep, her breathing slow and even. Jaskier was asleep before the candle burned down an inch, because Jaskier could sleep through anything including his own bad choices.

For a while there was only the rain and the page-turning.

"You're not sleeping," she said.

"Neither are you."

"I'm reading."

"You've been on the same page for twenty minutes."

A pause. The candle guttered in a draft from the window.

"I'm thinking," she said.

"About what."

She looked at him. In the low light her eyes were deep purple, the color they'd been when he first met her in Rinde, and for a disorienting moment he was back there — before all of it, before Ciri, before the djinn and its mess of wishes, back when Yennefer was just the most dangerous thing in a town full of dangerous things.

"Whether this is better or worse than being alone," she said.

He sat up. The floor creaked. Across the room Jaskier snored.

"Which is it?"

She looked at him for a long time. The rain came down. Ciri shifted in her sleep, murmuring something in Elder Speech.

"I haven't decided yet," Yennefer said finally, and something about the *yet* landed in his chest like it meant to stay there.

He didn't sleep for a long time. Neither, he was fairly certain, did she.

:::end-chapter
