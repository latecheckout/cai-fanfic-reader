:::chapter Gravity's Edge

Kepler Station hung at the edge of everything, a spinning ring of metal and light suspended between the known galaxy and the vast dark beyond. Zara had called it home for all eighteen years of her life, and she'd never once set foot on a planet.

"You're not even a little curious?" Jin floated beside her in the observation dome, their reflection ghosting across the stars. "Earth is right there. Three weeks on a transport and you could feel real gravity."

"This is real gravity." Zara gestured at the station's curved floor, the gentle pull of centrifugal force that her body had never known anything else. "Why would I want to feel heavy?"

Jin laughed—they always laughed at her station-born stubbornness. They'd grown up on Mars, had felt soil beneath their feet, had known a sky that changed colors instead of endless black. They'd arrived at Kepler six months ago for the engineering apprenticeship, and Zara still didn't understand why anyone would choose to leave a planet.

"Because it's different," Jin said. "Because there's more to existence than maintenance schedules and recycled air."

"There's the stars."

"Which you watch but never visit."

The words stung because they were true. Zara had tracked ships leaving Kepler since she was old enough to understand what leaving meant. She'd memorized their trajectories, calculated their fuel consumption, dreamed their destinations. But she'd never requested a berth.

"My parents need me here," she said. The old excuse, worn smooth with use.

"Your parents want you to be happy." Jin turned to face her, and in the starlight their expression was unreadable. "I've seen how you look at the departure logs. I've seen the applications you start but never send."

"You hacked my personal files?"

"Please. You leave them open on the shared terminal." Jin's smile softened. "There's an opening on the Magellan. Deep space survey, two years out. They need an environmental systems specialist."

Zara's heart clenched. The Magellan. The actual edge of human exploration. She'd followed their mission reports for years.

"I couldn't."

"You could." Jin pressed something into her hand—a data chip. "I submitted an application on your behalf. They want an interview."

"Jin—"

"You can be angry later. Right now, just—look." They gestured at the endless dark beyond the dome. "The universe is so much bigger than this station. You've spent eighteen years preparing. Maybe it's time to stop watching the ships leave and get on one."

Zara looked at the chip, then at the stars, then at Jin's hopeful face. The station hummed around her, familiar and safe and suddenly, unbearably small.

"Okay," she whispered. "I'll do the interview."

Jin's grin lit up brighter than any star.

:::end-chapter

:::chapter The Interview

The Magellan's captain had eyes like deep space—dark and vast and slightly terrifying. Captain Okonkwo conducted the interview via holo-link, her image flickering in the station's ancient communication bay.

"Your application says you've never left Kepler Station," she said. "That's unusual for someone who wants to spend two years in the deep black."

Zara gripped the edge of her chair, hidden from the camera's view. "I've studied every environmental system in the fleet. I know the Magellan's life support better than some of your crew."

"Knowing a ship isn't the same as living in one." Okonkwo's gaze was measuring. "Space is different when you can't go home. When the nearest station is months away. When the only gravity you feel is what you make."

"With respect, Captain, I've never known anything else." Zara heard her voice steadying, finding confidence she didn't know she had. "For me, the Magellan wouldn't be leaving home. It would be finding one."

The captain was silent for a long moment. Then something shifted in her expression—not quite a smile, but close.

"We launch in three weeks," she said. "Your parents will need to sign the waiver. You're still technically a minor in Earth-standard years."

"I—" Zara's breath caught. "I'm in?"

"Provisionally. Jin's recommendation carried weight. They said you were the best systems analyst they'd ever worked with." Okonkwo's image flickered. "Don't make them a liar."

The connection closed. Zara sat in the empty comm bay, her heart pounding so hard she could feel it in her fingertips. Three weeks. In three weeks, she'd leave everything she'd ever known.

She ran to find Jin.

:::end-chapter

:::chapter The Edge of Everything

The night before the Magellan's departure, Zara and Jin floated in the observation dome one last time. Kepler Station spun slowly beneath them, its lights like a galaxy in miniature.

"You're scared," Jin said. Not a question.

"Terrified." Zara watched her home rotate, seeing it differently now—smaller than it had seemed when it was her whole world. "What if I hate it? What if I can't adapt?"

"What if you love it? What if you find exactly what you've been looking for?" Jin nudged her gently. "The what-ifs go both ways."

"You're annoyingly optimistic."

"Someone has to be."

They floated in comfortable silence. Zara thought about everything she was leaving—her parents' quarters, the hydroponics bay where she'd spent childhood afternoons, the precise hum of systems she'd memorized before she could read.

"I'm not coming back," she said suddenly. "Not permanently. Even if I hate the Magellan. I know that now."

Jin nodded. "The universe is too big to stay in one place. Especially when you've spent your whole life watching everyone else leave."

"Is that why you came to Kepler? To understand people like me?"

"I came to Kepler because it was the farthest posting I could find." Jin's smile was complicated. "Sometimes you have to go to the edge of everything to figure out where you belong."

The departure alarm sounded, soft and insistent. Zara's transport shuttle was boarding.

She hugged Jin—carefully, in zero-g—and felt something between sadness and exhilaration settle in her chest. This was the moment she'd imagined for eighteen years, and it felt nothing like she'd expected. It felt like beginning.

"Send me pictures," Jin said. "Of everything. Stars I've never seen, planets with no names yet, the edge of the known galaxy."

"I will." Zara pushed off toward the airlock. "And Jin? Thank you. For making me brave enough to stop watching and start going."

Jin's reply was lost to the hiss of the airlock, but Zara saw them wave through the porthole. Then the shuttle was moving, and Kepler Station was shrinking behind her, and ahead was nothing but stars and darkness and the vast, unknown everything she'd been born to explore.

:::end-chapter
