:::chapter Chapter 1: Ghost in the System

The migraine hit V like a mono-wire through the skull.

They clutched the edge of the bathroom sink, knuckles white against the cold ceramic, and watched their reflection flicker in the mirror. For a moment—just a fraction of a second—they saw Johnny staring back at them. Same face, different eyes. Different everything.

"You look like shit," Johnny said, materializing beside them in that way he had, like static resolving into signal. He was leaning against the wall, arms crossed, cigarette dangling from his lips despite being a goddamn hallucination who couldn't actually smoke.

"Feel like it too." V splashed water on their face. It didn't help. Nothing helped anymore. The Relic was eating them alive from the inside out, and every day they woke up was another day borrowed from a future that was rapidly running out.

Night City glittered through the window of their megabuilding apartment. A million lights, a million lives, a million people who didn't know or care that one more soul was fighting a losing battle against obsolescence. That was the thing about this city—it consumed everything. Dreams, ambitions, people. It took what it wanted and left the husks behind.

"You're getting morbid," Johnny observed. "That's my job."

"Maybe you're rubbing off on me."

"That's what I'm afraid of."

V turned to look at him—really look at him. The construct, the engram, the ghost in their machine. Johnny Silverhand, terrorist and rock star, dead for over fifty years and somehow more alive than half the people V knew. He was a memory given form, a pattern of ones and zeros that had somehow developed opinions about V's choice of breakfast cereal.

"What do you want, Johnny?"

"Same thing I always want. To burn this whole rotten system to the ground." He took a drag of his non-existent cigarette. "But right now? Right now I want you to stop feeling sorry for yourself and get moving. We've got a lead on Mikoshi, remember? Clock's ticking."

The clock was always ticking. That was the problem.

V pushed off from the sink and walked into the main room. Their apartment was small—just a bed, a desk, a window overlooking the chaos of Watson. Judy had offered them a place at her new commune, somewhere outside the city, somewhere green and quiet and nothing like the concrete jungle they'd grown up in. V had said no. They couldn't leave. Not yet. Not until this was finished, one way or another.

Their phone buzzed. A message from Panam, checking in. Another from Viktor, reminding them about their appointment. A third from an unknown number that was probably Rogue, operating through one of her many proxy accounts.

Everyone wanted something from V. Everyone had a job, a favor, a plea for help. And V helped them all, because what else was there to do? In a city full of monsters, being useful was the only currency that mattered.

"You know what your problem is?" Johnny said, following them like the ghost he was.

"I've got a dying brain and a terrorist in my head?"

"Besides that. You care too much. You're trying to save everyone when you can't even save yourself."

"Someone has to."

"No. They don't. That's the lie this city sells—that if you just try hard enough, work hard enough, sacrifice enough, you can make a difference. But you can't. The game is rigged. Always has been. The corps own everything, and the rest of us are just dancing to their tune."

V stopped at the window, looking out at the skyline. Arasaka Tower dominated the horizon, a monument to everything wrong with the world. They'd been inside that tower. They'd seen what lay beneath the corporate propaganda—the greed, the cruelty, the casual disregard for human life. And yet the tower still stood, and the corps still ran the world, and nothing ever really changed.

"Then why fight at all?" V asked.

Johnny was quiet for a long moment. When he spoke, his voice was different. Softer. Almost human.

"Because fighting is all we've got. Because even if we can't win, we can make the bastards work for their victory. Because maybe—just maybe—if enough people refuse to play by their rules, something breaks. Something changes. Maybe not in our lifetime. Maybe not in the next. But someday."

V turned to look at him. The terrorist. The idealist. The man who'd nuked a building and killed thousands in the name of freedom.

"That's not very punk rock of you, Johnny. Talking about the future like that."

Johnny smiled. It was the closest thing to genuine V had ever seen on his face.

"Yeah, well. You're a bad influence on me."

:::chapter Chapter 2: The Cracks

Three days later, V flatlined for the first time.

It happened in the middle of a gig—simple job, grab and go, nothing that should have been dangerous. One moment they were cracking a security terminal, the next they were on the floor, seizing, the world dissolving into static and noise.

When they came back, Johnny was sitting cross-legged on the floor beside them, looking more shaken than V had ever seen him.

"Don't ever do that again," he said.

"Wasn't exactly planned." V pushed themselves up, wincing at the pain behind their eyes. Their neural interface was throwing error messages, red warnings scrolling across their vision. "How long was I out?"

"Three minutes. Felt longer." Johnny stood, his movements jerkier than usual. "The Relic's degrading faster than we thought. Vik's projections were optimistic."

"Vik's always optimistic. That's why I keep going to him."

V made it to their feet, steadying themselves against the wall. The target data was still on their neural storage—they hadn't lost that, at least. But their hands were shaking, and when they looked at Johnny, they saw something unfamiliar in his expression.

Fear.

"We need to move faster," Johnny said. "Mikoshi, the engram data, all of it. We're running out of time."

"We've been running out of time since this thing went in my head."

"Yeah, but now I actually believe it."

They finished the job. Got paid. Went home to the apartment that felt smaller every day. V stood at the window again, watching the neon blur of Night City, and tried not to think about how few nights they might have left.

"For what it's worth," Johnny said, "I'm sorry."

V turned. "What?"

"This whole situation. The Relic, the overwrite, all of it. None of it would be happening if some gonk hadn't stolen my engram from Arasaka." He laughed bitterly. "Of course, if they hadn't, I'd still be a construct in a vault somewhere, not even aware I existed. So maybe there's no good outcome here."

"There's never a good outcome in Night City. Just outcomes you can live with."

"And some you can't."

They stood in silence for a while. Outside, the city pulsed with its usual rhythm—violence and commerce and desperation, all wrapped in pretty lights. Somewhere out there, people were being born and dying and falling in love and getting zeroed, a million stories playing out simultaneously, most of them tragic.

"If I don't make it," V said finally, "I want you to know something."

"V—"

"Let me finish. If I don't make it, and you somehow get control of this body... don't waste it. Don't spend the rest of your life trying to burn things down. Build something instead. Help someone. Be better than you were."

Johnny was quiet for a long moment.

"That's a lot to ask of a dead terrorist."

"Yeah, well. Consider it my dying wish."

Another silence. Then Johnny nodded, once, like a promise being made.

"I'll try," he said. "No guarantees. But I'll try."

V turned back to the window, and together they watched the city that might be their grave burn bright against the endless night.

:::chapter Chapter 3: Human After All

In the end, it came down to a choice.

V stood in the heart of Mikoshi, surrounded by the data of ten thousand souls, and listened while Alt Cunningham—what was left of her—explained their options. Take the body back, live a few more months, let Johnny fade away. Or let Johnny have the body, retreat into the Net, become something not quite alive and not quite dead.

Neither option was good. But that was Night City for you. No happy endings, just degrees of loss.

"What do you want?" V asked Johnny, their voice echoing in the digital void.

Johnny was quiet. He looked different here, in the pure data space—less solid, more like the ghost he'd always been. The terrorist and the rock star and the man who'd nuked a building, reduced to lines of code that might have been a soul.

"I want to live," he said finally. "But not at your expense. Not like this."

"That's not an answer."

"Yeah, well, I've never been good at answers." He laughed, but there was no humor in it. "You know what's funny? I spent my whole life fighting. Fighting Arasaka, fighting the system, fighting anyone who tried to tell me what to do. I thought that made me strong. Turns out, it just made me alone."

V waited.

"Then I ended up in your head," Johnny continued. "And I had to watch you. Watch you help people, care about people, build relationships I never could. And I realized... all that fighting? It was just another way of running. Running from the parts of myself I didn't want to face."

"So what do you want now?"

Johnny looked at them—really looked, with eyes that held fifty years of regret and something that might have been hope.

"I want you to live, V. Go back to your body, spend whatever time you've got left doing something that matters. And when it's over... maybe I'll find you on the other side. If there is one."

V felt tears they didn't think they could cry in this digital space.

"You're going to sacrifice yourself? You?"

"Don't get sentimental. It doesn't suit either of us." But Johnny was smiling, and for once it looked genuine. "Besides, I've been dead for fifty years. What's a little more nothing?"

They embraced—a strange thing, two consciousnesses wrapping around each other in a space without bodies. But it felt real. It felt like goodbye.

"Thank you," V whispered. "For everything."

"Don't thank me yet. You've still got to make it count."

Then Johnny was gone, and V was falling back toward their body, toward the world, toward whatever came next.

When they woke up, the sun was rising over Night City. It was still a hellhole. Still corrupt, still broken, still eating itself alive. But V was alive too, for however long that lasted, and they had work to do.

Johnny Silverhand was gone. But something of him remained—a memory, a voice, a reminder that even the worst of us can choose to be better in the end.

V got up, put on their jacket, and walked out into the new day.
