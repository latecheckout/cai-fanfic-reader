:::chapter Hawkins to Columbus, 472 Miles

The car was packed so tightly that Steve could only see Robin's eyes in the rearview mirror. Everything else — three suitcases, a box of tapes, a lamp Robin had insisted on bringing despite Steve's reasoned argument that colleges had electricity — had formed a wall between them and anything that might be called rear visibility.

"This is a death trap," Steve said.

"This is an adventure," Robin said. She had a map open across her lap, which was either very charming or very 1986 of her, depending on how you felt about people who printed out MapQuest directions. "Turn left in four miles."

"I know the way."

"You said that about Hawkins to Indianapolis and we ended up in Terre Haute."

"There was construction."

"There was you being too proud to ask for directions."

Steve turned left in four miles.

---

They stopped at a rest area outside Columbus because Robin had had three coffees since Hawkins and the bladder of a normal-sized person. Steve stood in the parking lot and watched the cars pass and thought about how weird it was to be going somewhere with a plan attached. A plan that didn't involve the Upside Down. A plan that was just: show up, unpack, be a college student.

He was twenty. He'd been twenty for two months. He'd spent those two months at Family Video, driving kids to the lake, and having the same conversation with his mom about how he'd figure it out.

He was figuring it out. This was the figuring.

"You look constipated," Robin said, appearing at his elbow with a bag of pretzels she'd acquired from somewhere.

"I'm thinking."

"About what."

"About the fact that I'm going to college. Like, that I'm actually doing this."

Robin ate a pretzel. "You passed the entrance exam. You got accepted. You saved up enough money—"

"I know the facts."

"So what's the problem."

Steve looked at her. Robin looked back. She had her hair down today, which she almost never did — it fell to her shoulders in a wave that made her look softer than she usually let herself look in Hawkins, where being soft felt like something you did at your own risk.

"What if I'm not—" He stopped. Started again. "What if I show up and it turns out I'm just, like. A guy who was good at staying alive. And that doesn't make you interesting at college."

Robin was quiet for a moment. A truck rumbled past on the highway, and the pretzel bag crinkled.

"Steve," she said. "You taught yourself three wilderness survival techniques last winter because Dustin mentioned it might be useful, and then you taught them to Dustin, and then you made laminated reference cards for everyone in the group. You read books because you're bored and then you get mad when no one else has read them. You make the world's most unnecessary amount of eye contact with everyone you meet because you actually care whether they're okay. You're genuinely kind to people who don't think you're going to be, which is more interesting than anything I've seen anybody else do."

He stared at her.

"Also you still take care of the kids," she added, "even when they don't need it and would definitely be fine without you, because you love them, and you're too proud to say it, but you do."

"I—yeah."

"You're going to be fine," Robin said. "And if it turns out you're not, I will be three buildings over in the music building and you can come find me and we can eat terrible dining hall food and I'll help you figure it out."

Steve looked at her for a long time. The sun was doing something nice to the highway, turning everything gold and distant.

"Okay," he said.

"Okay," she agreed.

They got back in the car. Robin refolded the map incorrectly. Steve said nothing about it, which was, he was starting to understand, its own kind of love.
