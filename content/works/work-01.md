:::chapter Before the Bridge
:::summary
Vi makes a choice in the last seconds. Powder is still falling.
:::end-summary

The explosion had a sound Vi would never stop hearing.

Not the crack of the hex crystal or the shriek of metal — those were just noise. The sound that mattered happened in the space between the detonation and the aftermath: Powder's voice, small and certain, saying *I made it work, Vi. I finally made it work.*

She didn't hear it over the blast. She heard it in the weeks after, when she was still alive and everyone else wasn't, playing on repeat in the dark.

---

Enforcer custody was clean. That was the thing Vi hadn't expected. Scrubbed floors and medical inspections and three meals a day that arrived on schedule. After the Undercity, where everything was negotiated and nothing was guaranteed, the rigid efficiency of Stillwater felt like a joke she wasn't in on.

She spent the first week waiting for the conditions. The trade. The *actually*.

They never came. Just gray walls and counting days and learning, slowly, that grief was not a single thing but a collection of small collapses — a sudden smell, a particular quality of light, the shape of a hand reaching out.

She made herself remember Powder's face as it had been before. Braids and freckles and the absolute certainty of someone who had not yet learned that the world would not bend to accommodate her hope.

She made herself remember it on purpose, because forgetting felt like the real death.

:::chapter The Weight of a Name
:::summary
Seven years. Jinx is not a ghost. She is something worse — she is present.
:::end-summary

The first time Vi saw the name JINX painted across a burned-out chem-barons' warehouse in thirty-foot letters, she stopped walking for a full minute.

Around her, Undercity foot traffic parted and reformed like water around a stone. Nobody stared. Staring at things you shouldn't acknowledge was a fast way to become one of them.

"You know her?" Ekko appeared at her elbow with the specific casualness of someone who had been watching her reaction.

"I know who she was."

"That's not what I asked."

Vi started walking again. Ekko matched her pace without being invited to. She'd been back three days and he'd already annexed himself into her orbit with the comfortable authority of someone who'd kept the fire going while she was gone and felt this entitled him to things.

It probably did.

"She blew up the Progress Day gala," Vi said. "Killed a Council member. She's — " She stopped. *She's Powder* sat in her throat and wouldn't move. "She's out of control."

"She's been out of control for six years." Ekko's voice carried something measured. Careful. The voice of a person who had grieved someone and made their peace with it. "The Jinx thing was her. The name, the identity, all of it. She chose it."

"People choose things when they don't have better options."

"Vi." He stopped walking. Made her stop with him by sheer refusal to move. "What are you planning to do?"

She looked at the name on the wall. The letters were slightly uneven, the A tilting left, the way Powder had always held her brush when she was concentrating — right hand, left lean, tongue just barely touching her lower lip.

"I'm going to find her," she said. "After that I'll figure it out."

:::chapter Powder Would Have Understood the Mechanism
:::notes-begin

:::notes-end

They found each other at the same time, which meant no one had the advantage of arrival.

Vi had been following a chain of explosions — not random, the pattern was there if you knew where to look, an escalating signature that felt like a message in a cipher she'd helped invent. Powder had been following Vi the same way, tracking her through secondhand reports and one surveillance photograph that Vi had already identified as bait.

They met in the old underbelly pump station. The one that had been their hiding spot when they were eight and the Enforcers were sweeping the lane.

Powder — *Jinx*, the name pressed against Vi's teeth — was standing in the middle of the room with her arms at her sides and her expression doing something Vi had never seen on her face before. Not happiness. Not rage. Something quiet and demolished and afraid.

"You came," Powder said.

"I came."

"I didn't think you'd—" She stopped. Started again. "I blew up a lot of things."

"Yeah." Vi walked toward her slowly, the way she'd learned to approach injured animals, everything telegraphed and nothing sudden. "I noticed."

"I thought maybe—" Powder's hands came up and then dropped. "I didn't think you'd want to find me after."

Vi stopped in front of her. Close enough to touch, not touching yet. Powder was taller than she remembered. She'd forgotten that bodies kept growing even when everything else stopped.

"I want a lot of things," Vi said. "Some of them are complicated. But finding you was not complicated. Finding you was the only thing that wasn't."

Powder looked at her for a long time.

"I'm not who I was," she said.

"I know."

"I've done things—"

"I know."

"You can't just—"

"Powder." Vi said the name deliberately, watched it land, watched Powder flinch and then hold very still as though waiting to see if it hurt or healed. "I'm not here to fix you. I'm not here to pretend none of it happened. I'm here because you're my sister and I was gone for seven years and I'm not going to be gone anymore."

The silence stretched.

Then Powder sat down, suddenly, cross-legged on the floor like she had when she was small, and put her face in her hands, and Vi sat down across from her, and neither of them spoke for a long time.

It wasn't a beginning. It was something stranger and harder than that — two people in the ruins of something that had been, trying to figure out what it could become.

Vi had fought worse odds.
