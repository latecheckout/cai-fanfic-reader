:::chapter Three Pipe Problem

The flat on Baker Street smelled of violin rosin and last night's experiment, and John had long since stopped being bothered by either.

He sat in his chair with his tea and watched Sherlock stand at the window. Sherlock was doing the thing he did when he was thinking — perfectly still, hands pressed together, chin resting on his fingertips, eyes fixed on the middle distance where London went about its oblivious business. The violin was in the corner, abandoned mid-phrase as whatever case he was working had suddenly come clear at two in the morning and stolen his attention from the music.

John had heard the moment it happened. A single sustained note, and then silence, and then the quiet footsteps crossing the sitting room, and Sherlock standing where he was now, not moving, for the past forty minutes.

The tea had gone cold. John didn't get up to make another.

---

"You're staring," Sherlock said, without turning.

"Observing," John said. "There's a difference."

A pause. Something shifted in Sherlock's stillness — not much, a slight recalibration.

"The difference being."

"I'm not trying to deduce anything. I'm just looking."

Another pause. Longer this time.

"People don't generally look at me without wanting something from it," Sherlock said. His voice was careful in a way it wasn't when he was confident. Careful in the way that meant he was working something out and hadn't finished.

John thought about this. About the number of times he'd watched Sherlock move through a crime scene, touch things, see things, reconstruct entire histories from ash and shoe leather. About the way it still seemed like a magic trick he'd never entirely gotten used to. About how the wanting had stopped being about the trick a long time ago, if it had ever been about that.

"No," he said. "I just—" He stopped. Started again. "You go very still when you're thinking. I hadn't noticed that before. How long it lasts."

Sherlock turned.

In the gray light from the window, his face was doing something John couldn't fully read — which was unusual, because he'd learned to read Sherlock reasonably well over the years, which mostly meant learning to read the spaces where Sherlock edited himself, the micro-expressions that passed through before the mask came down.

There was no mask right now. That was the odd thing. Just Sherlock looking at him with the particular attentiveness he usually reserved for interesting evidence.

"You've been watching me for forty minutes," Sherlock said.

"Yes."

"Without wanting anything."

"I didn't say that," John said, and then stopped, because that was probably too much.

Sherlock went very still. Differently still than before — not the stillness of thinking but the stillness of something else. Something that John's medical training told him involved a slightly elevated heart rate and a particular kind of attention.

Mrs. Hudson called up the stairs about parcels. The moment broke like a soap bubble.

Sherlock turned back to the window.

John got up to get his cold tea and thought about how it was probably fine. How probably nothing needed to happen. How some equilibria were worth keeping exactly as they were.

He thought this with great conviction, right up until Sherlock said, still facing the window: "Forty-three minutes. In case you were counting."

"I wasn't," John said.

"No," Sherlock agreed, and John could hear the something in his voice, the careful, considered something that Sherlock would have called data. "You were just looking."

John went to make fresh tea. He stayed in the kitchen longer than necessary.

When he came back, Sherlock was playing the violin again. The phrase he'd abandoned was picked up and carried forward, and John sat in his chair, and neither of them said anything, and the afternoon settled around them in the particular way of Baker Street, which was to say: not finished, and not needing to be.

:::end-chapter
