:::chapter UTC-3
:::summary
Brazil. Year two. A 2am call.
:::end-summary

Hinata's phone lit up at 2:17 AM local time, which was 2:17 PM in Miyagi, which meant Kageyama was calling from wherever he went after practice when he wanted to pretend he wasn't checking in.

Hinata answered on the second ring. He'd been awake. He was always awake at 2am in Brazil, because Brazil operated on a different relationship with night than Japan did, louder and warmer and pushing against his schedule in ways he'd mostly stopped fighting.

"Hey," he said.

"You're awake," Kageyama said, which was his version of *are you okay*.

"Couldn't sleep. How was practice?"

"Fine." A pause. "Oikawa-san watched from the stands for the second time this week."

Hinata sat up, cross-legged on his too-small bed. "What did he say?"

"Nothing. He just watched." A longer pause. "It was annoying."

"It means you're interesting to watch," Hinata said.

"I know that." There was something almost-indignant in Kageyama's voice, the familiar tone of someone who had been given a compliment and didn't know what to do with it. "I just don't like—"

"Being observed," Hinata supplied. "I know."

Silence on the line. Not uncomfortable silence — they'd stopped having uncomfortable silences about a year ago, around the time Hinata realized that Kageyama's silence was a form of presence, not absence. When Kageyama didn't talk it usually meant he was thinking something he didn't have words for yet.

"How's your shoulder," Kageyama said finally.

"Better. The physical therapist says two more weeks."

"Two weeks until what."

"Until I can spike at full power again." Hinata looked at the ceiling fan, which was spinning lazily in the humid air. "Coach Romero is already drawing up combinations."

Another silence.

"Combinations with who," Kageyama said.

Hinata smiled at the ceiling. "With Marcelo, probably. He's got the best set timing on the team after—" He stopped.

"After what," Kageyama said, and his voice had gone very careful.

"After you," Hinata said. "Obviously."

He could hear Kageyama breathe. Heard the way the careful quality in his voice shifted into something else, something that Hinata had been tracking for two years from five thousand miles away, cataloguing its appearances and trying to name it.

"Go to sleep," Kageyama said finally. "You have morning practice."

"I know." Hinata lay back down. "Kageyama."

"What."

"I set a new personal record on receives today. Coach said it was the best he'd seen from me all season."

A pause. Then, in a voice that tried very hard to be neutral: "I know. Sugawara-san sent me the clip."

Hinata laughed. He laughed for real, not performing it, just the genuine thing that happened in his chest when something was exactly what he'd hoped for. "You've been talking to Suga-san?"

"He sends things sometimes. I don't ask him to."

"Sure," Hinata said. "Good night, Kageyama."

"Good night," Kageyama said, and the call ended, and Hinata lay in his too-small Brazilian bed and stared at the ceiling and thought about how the word *obviously* was doing a lot of work lately.

:::chapter The Clip
:::notes-begin
The clip in question is a perfectly reasonable thing that a teammate would send to another teammate. There is nothing significant about it. Kageyama has watched it eleven times.
:::notes-end

Kageyama watched the clip eleven times.

This was not excessive. This was analysis. He was identifying the mechanics of Hinata's receive form, which had changed subtly in the year and a half since they'd been on the same court, the angles refined, the footwork more economical. It was professionally relevant information.

He watched it a twelfth time.

In the clip, Hinata dove for a ball that was already past him by any reasonable measure, redirected it in midair with a twist that shouldn't have been physically possible, and popped it perfectly to the two-meter line. Then he landed, rolled, came up on his feet, and grinned at his setter with the specific wild joy he'd always had — the joy that said the game was infinite and so was he.

Kageyama knew that grin. He'd been on the receiving end of it more times than he could count, usually right after they'd pulled off something impossible together. It felt different, watching it happen with someone else.

He was aware that this feeling had a name. He'd been declining to name it.

His phone buzzed. Sugawara: *seen it?*

Kageyama: *yes*

Sugawara: *and?*

Kageyama: *his receive form is improving significantly*

Sugawara: *[laughing emoji] [laughing emoji] you are the most obvious person I have ever met and I coached Daichi*

Kageyama stared at his phone.

Kageyama: *what does that mean*

Sugawara: *it means you should call him more*

Kageyama: *I call him*

Sugawara: *you call him when you can't sleep and then you talk about volleyball for 45 minutes and you think that's enough*

Kageyama: *we also talk about other things*

Sugawara: *tobio*

The use of his given name. From Sugawara it was not disciplinary, it was something softer — the tone that meant he was being seen, specifically and clearly, by someone who'd known him since he was still learning what it meant to play with other people.

Kageyama put his phone down. Picked it up again.

He watched the clip one more time.
