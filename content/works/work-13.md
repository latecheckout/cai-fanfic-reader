:::chapter 01: What Muscle Memory Knows
:::notes-begin
Set approximately four months after the end of season 6 / the Final Act. Manga spoilers are present but handled gently. If you're anime only and want to remain so, this fic has some details that could be considered spoilers for where the manga goes — use your judgment.
:::notes-end

Midoriya Izuku had been taking notes his entire life.

Quirks, movements, strategies, relationships, weaknesses, potential — fifteen notebooks so far, the newest half-full, all of them catalogued with a specificity that his classmates found either impressive or slightly alarming depending on whether they'd seen what he wrote about them.

The new notebook had a different first entry.

*Things I am allowed to want,* he'd written at the top of page one, on a day when Aizawa-sensei had said something during debriefing that lodged in his chest and wouldn't leave. *Things I am allowed to want for myself.*

The rest of the page was blank.

He'd been staring at it for three weeks.

---

Todoroki found him in the common room at midnight, which was not unusual — they'd both been bad sleepers since the battle, had learned each other's late-night patterns the way you learned the rhythms of someone you spent a great deal of time almost talking to.

"You're not asleep," Todoroki said, which was Todoroki's way of saying *are you okay* in the same way that *it's raining* was Todoroki's way of saying *I'm thinking about you*.

Midoriya had learned to translate.

"Neither are you," he said.

Todoroki sat down at the other end of the couch, the careful amount of space that had always been his default and had been, lately, getting slightly less careful. Midoriya had noticed this. He was a professional noticer.

"The notebook is blank," Todoroki observed.

"Top of the page has writing."

"The rest is blank." Todoroki looked at it with the focused attention he gave to things he was trying to understand. "What does it say at the top."

Midoriya looked at the page. Thought about whether to answer. The notebook was facing away from Todoroki anyway — he couldn't see.

"Things I'm allowed to want," he said.

A pause.

"And you can't fill it in," Todoroki said.

"I keep trying. It doesn't—" He stopped. "Every time I write something it feels selfish. Like I'm taking up space."

Todoroki was quiet. Not empty quiet — the quiet he did when he was choosing words.

"My father," he said finally, "told me I existed for a purpose. That purpose was not mine. It was his." He paused. "I've been — relearning. What it means to have a purpose that belongs to me."

Midoriya looked at him. Todoroki was looking at the notebook.

"What have you put in yours," Midoriya said. "If you have one."

"I don't write it down," Todoroki said. "I think about it when I'm training. When I'm using the left side." A slight, barely-there thing in his expression — not a smile, but something adjacent. "I thought about Endeavor for a long time when I used the left side. Now I think about other things."

"What things."

Todoroki looked at him.

The common room was very quiet. The lights were set to low, emergency-level, and in that light Todoroki's face was half-shadow, which was the thing about Todoroki that you eventually stopped noticing but that was always there: the left and the right, the fire and the ice, the split right down the middle and the person who had been living inside that split and learning to occupy the whole of himself.

"What I want," Todoroki said. "The list I haven't written down."

Midoriya's pen was still in his hand. He looked down at the blank page.

"Okay," he said.

"Write something down," Todoroki said. "It doesn't have to be big."

Midoriya wrote: *to sleep eight hours*.

Todoroki read it upside down. Made a sound that was almost a laugh.

"That's a good one," he said.

"Your turn," Midoriya said.

Todoroki thought for a moment. Then he reached over, carefully, the distance between them slightly less than it had been — took the pen from Midoriya's hand with fingers that were room-temperature, neither burning nor cold — and wrote, below the first line: *to be understood correctly, the first time, without having to explain*.

He handed the pen back. Their fingers touched for a moment longer than necessary.

Midoriya looked at the page. At the two lines of handwriting, his cramped and urgent, Todoroki's careful and even.

"I think you'd like to have a list," Midoriya said.

"Maybe," Todoroki said. "Notebooks aren't really my thing."

"There are other ways."

"I know." He looked at the window, the campus grounds dim and quiet outside. "I'm working on it."

Midoriya wrote a third line: *to know what I'm working toward, not just who I'm working for*.

They stayed until 2am, the notebook slowly filling, trading it back and forth without discussing the fact that they were doing this — two people learning to want things, in the quiet middle of the night, in the common room of UA, in the long year after the battle.

:::chapter 02: Bakugou's Version

Bakugou would not say that he was watching Midoriya and Todoroki. That was weird and creepy and none of his business.

He was simply aware of them. This was different. He was aware of most things. It was a training thing.

The training thing, however, did not explain why he'd noticed the notebook, or the way Midoriya kept looking at it with the expression he'd had since they were kids when something wouldn't come right, the expression that meant he was chasing something he couldn't quite catch yet.

Or the way Todoroki had started positioning himself in Midoriya's orbit. Not dramatically. Nothing Todoroki did was dramatic, which was honestly offensive given the visual. Just — there. Near. Within range.

Bakugou understood being near Midoriya. He didn't think about it in those terms and he never would but he understood it on a cellular level: Midoriya was the kind of person gravity worked differently around, had always been, from the moment he'd stood in front of something bigger than him with nothing but intention and gotten back up.

The thing Bakugou understood now that he hadn't understood when they were kids was that this was a gift, not an imposition.

He had been working on this understanding for approximately two years.

He was making progress.

"Deku," he said, on a Thursday in the training hall, when they were the last two left.

Midoriya looked up from where he was wrapping his hands.

"I'm watching your form on the Blackwhip extensions," Bakugou said. "It's getting sloppy."

"I know," Midoriya said.

"I can—" He stopped. Started again. "I can help you fix it. If you want."

Midoriya stared at him.

"Or not," Bakugou said. "I don't give a—"

"Yes," Midoriya said, quickly, before he could finish the retreat. "Yes. Please."

Bakugou nodded, once, sharply, the way he nodded when something was decided and he wasn't going to make it into a bigger deal than it was. Turned back toward the training floor.

Midoriya wrote something in his notebook later. Bakugou was not watching. He was simply aware.

The entry, if he had been watching, would have read: *to be seen by the person who knew you before, and have it be okay*.
