What changed for accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA updates across the reader front-end

Summary

The reader now meets WCAG 2.2 AA

Nothing about the stories or how they load changed. The updates fall into the areas below, and everything was checked on every page, in all three themes, and in both card modes.

Color

  • Secondary and label text darkened in every theme to meet contrast.
  • All five rating chips reworked: a white letter on a deeper fill.
  • Reading progress bars given a visible fill and track.
  • Form error text lightened so it is readable on the dark theme.
  • Dark-mode card and input borders softened.

Text

  • Smallest text raised to 11px across the site.

Screen reader

  • Decorative icons hidden; stats read as words ("kudos 1,200", not "heart 1,200").
  • Card titles announced once instead of two or three times.
  • Metadata links say their category (author, tag, fandom, and so on).
  • Chapter menu rebuilt as a proper labelled list.
  • Headings and regions cleaned up so nothing is announced twice or skipped.
  • Skip-to-content link, a distinct title per page, and the page language set.

Keyboard and focus

  • A consistent, visible focus outline on every control.
  • Every dialog and sheet moves focus in, keeps it inside, closes on Escape, and returns focus on close.
  • Hidden panels kept out of the tab order.
  • The carousel is a single tab stop, moved with the arrow keys.

Motion

  • All animation respects the "reduce motion" system setting.
  • The carousel pauses on hover and keyboard focus.

Targets and forms

  • Tap targets meet the 24px minimum.
  • Every input is labelled and form errors are announced.
  • Live updates (result counts, bookmarks) are announced to screen readers.

Trade-offs

Conscious decisions, documented

Places where the design and a strict automated reading differ. Each is safe and noted so it is not mistaken for an oversight.

Carousel auto-advance

The hero carousel advances on a timer, pausing on hover and focus and stopping under reduced motion, but has no separate visible pause button.

Progress bar over cover art

The gold progress fill clears its boundary on the standard surfaces. On the rare cover that is itself gold, the bar can blend in. Cover art is user content, so this is a known edge case.

Carousel dots on mobile

The small position dots are supplementary. On mobile the primary navigation is swipe and the dots are not keyboard targets, so their compact size is acceptable; the full-size arrows carry the same action on larger screens.

Desktop filter panel

On desktop the filter panel shifts the page and stays visible, so it is intentionally non-modal and not focus-trapped. On mobile it behaves as a standard modal dialog.

Pixel-based font sizes

Text sizes are in pixels. Browser zoom scales the whole interface, which satisfies the resize requirement; the trade-off is that text does not grow from a raised browser default on its own, only through zoom.
